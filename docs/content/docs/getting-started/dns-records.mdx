---
title: DNS records
description: Generate and publish the A, PTR, MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records for your domain.
---

Other mail servers decide whether to accept your mail from your DNS records. Set up the PTR record and your `INCOMING_MAIL_HOST` and `MAIL_SERVER_IP` values first, then generate the rest.

## Generate the records

The easiest way is the setup script. It writes `server/records.json` for you.

```bash
chmod +x scripts/run.sh
./scripts/run.sh
```

To do it by hand, set `DOMAIN_NAME` and run the generator, `server/src/start.ts`. You can also change the domain in that file:

```ts
const records = new DNSRecordGenerator("example.com").generateAllRecords();
```

`records.json` contains one entry per record:

| Key | Contents |
|-----|----------|
| `SPF` | The SPF TXT record. |
| `DKIM` | `selector`, the public `dkimRecord` to publish, and the `privateKey`. |
| `DMARC` | The DMARC TXT record. |
| `MX` | The MX record that points at `INCOMING_MAIL_HOST`. |

<Callout title="records.json contains your DKIM private key" type="error">
  Never commit it or share it. It is in `.gitignore`. Publish only the records themselves in DNS, and keep the private key in `DKIM_PRIVATE_KEY`.
</Callout>

## Essential records

![The A and PTR records to create](/images/dns-records.png)

### A record

- Create `mail.example.com` (and `mx.example.com`) pointing to your server's IP address.
- Make sure the hostname matches your SSL certificate.

### PTR record (reverse DNS)

- Ask your hosting provider to set it. You cannot set it in your own DNS.
- It must match your mail server's hostname.

### MX record

Share your mail server's hostname with anyone who wants to use your server, and tell them to create an MX record that points at it.

- Point it at your mail server hostname.
- Set a priority, for example `10 mx.example.com`.

## Anti-spam records

![SPF, DKIM and DMARC records](/images/anti-spam-records.png)

### SPF

Lists the servers that may send mail for your domain. The generator creates:

```text
v=spf1 a mx ip4:YOUR_IP ~all
```

`~all` marks mail from other servers as suspicious. Once you are sure the record is right, change it to `-all` to tell receivers to reject it.

### DKIM

Adds a signature to every message so receivers can prove it came from you and was not changed. Publish the public key as a TXT record at:

```text
default._domainkey.example.com
```

The value looks like `v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=...`. The generator creates a 2048-bit RSA key. `default` is the selector, so it must match `DKIM_SELECTOR`. Put the private key from `records.json` in `DKIM_PRIVATE_KEY`.

### DMARC

Tells receivers what to do when SPF and DKIM checks fail, and where to send reports. Publish a TXT record at `_dmarc.example.com`. The generator creates:

```text
v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; pct=100; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com; ruf=mailto:dmarc-failures@example.com
```

Create the two report mailboxes (or change the addresses) so you receive the reports. Start with `p=none` or `p=quarantine` and move to `p=reject` when your reports are clean.

## Bounce domain

Only needed when you set `BOUNCE_DOMAIN` (see [Bounces](/docs/guides/bounces)). Pick a name such as `bounces.example.com` and give it:

| Type | Name | Value |
|------|------|-------|
| `MX` | `bounces.example.com` | `10 mx.example.com`, the server that receives your mail. |
| `TXT` | `bounces.example.com` | `v=spf1 ip4:YOUR_IP ~all` |

Return addresses look like `bounce+<id>@bounces.example.com`. Remote servers send their failure reports there, to port 25. The `TXT` record matters because receivers check SPF against the envelope sender, and from now on that is the bounce domain.
